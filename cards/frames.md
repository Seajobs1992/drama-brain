**Before a batch**
- Re-read the episode's script, its section in doc `locks`, and the previous episode's last frames.
- List every character, costume state, prop, wound, weather and time of day. Note what changed since the last episode.
- Write the scene map: compass directions, who stands where, the dialogue axis, where the light comes from.
- WIDES: put the scene master beside the location authority and the place's first wide shot from an earlier episode. A side-on master is not a street view. (LOCKED OUT 2-15 showed the wrong shop because this was skipped.)

**Every prompt**
- Image 1 is the scene master for place, layout and light. Then only the references the frame needs, each labelled.
- Name hands anatomically, LEFT or RIGHT, and say where they sit in the picture.
- An injury on a character's RIGHT brow sits over the eye nearer the LEFT edge of the picture. Write it that way.
- Over-the-shoulder: the foreground person is the LISTENER, on their correct side. Say who it is and what shows (hair, coat).
- One speaker per shot, face large enough for lip sync, eyeline to the listener's side of the axis.
- No text, numbers, logos or plates. Text is added in the edit.

**Check every image before anything depends on it**
- Face matches the approved identity image. Costume matches the sheet and the episode's state.
- Props: same design as the reference, correct hand, correct side, correct state. Count them (one mug, not two).
- Location matches the master: walls, doors, furniture, light, weather. Positions and eyelines match the neighbouring shots.
- Hands have five fingers. No extra people, no missing people, no text.
- Cannot confirm it in the image? It fails until confirmed. Never assume.
- Never flip or mirror. A defect gets one targeted correction grounded in the master, and a note of why.

**After**
- Contact board in shot order, corrected frames marked. Update doc `locks`. Full list: `brain.py section checklist "Checking every image"`.

## Learned the hard way
- 2026-11-20: Storyboard tools link only the speaking character, so over-the-shoulder frames lose the foreground person (the creator found it in Google's Storyboard Studio, 2026-11-20). Rule: any character with any visible part (shoulder, back of head, hand, legs at the edge) is linked with their reference and named in the frame prompt as the soft foreground, at a stated screen side, staying silent.
- 2026-11-20: An insert in Google's Storyboard Studio had no location reference (only Ruth, Dean, the sheet), so the desk drifted: two lamps, extra pen pot and folders, a different floor (The Cleaner, Shot 3, 2026-11-20). Rule: every frame, including inserts and close-ups of a surface, carries the scene's location reference; close views add no objects that are not in the location reference.
- 2026-11-20: Google refuses pictures and clips where a child is described by age, as vulnerable (barefoot, tiny), in a tight close-up, or in one frame with a threatening adult (e.g. a man at a sleeping child's bedside); and the video filter judges spoken threats (one line refused 14 times). Rules: name children, never age them; frame them medium or wider; keep a child and a threat in separate pictures (door, shadow, the object alone); show violence before or after, no blood; name tools by their honest job; never retry the same refused prompt; a refused spoken line goes back to the creator. Source: research/SAFETY-REWRITE.md (2026-11-20).
