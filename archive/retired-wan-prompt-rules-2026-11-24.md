# Retired Wan prompt rules (replaced on 24 Nov 2026 by WAN-3.0-CINEMATIC-DIALOGUE-STANDARD.md)
Kept for history only. Do not follow.

[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: WAN ENGLISH LIP SYNC SOLVED (user verdict 23 Nov 2026, tests T2 and T3 on clip 3c): Wan 3.0 reference-to-video lip syncs English on our Joseon faces when it is given REAL English speech to follow. T2: refs = the keyframe as image1 + an English reading of the lines as audio1; prompt 'First frame: use [@image1] as the first frame at the start of the video.' + the shot and camera + '[Timbre and dialogue] Extract the timbre features from [@audio1], and have her speak the following dialogue in English: "..."' + '[Lip sync and expression] The generated speech must precisely drive her lip movements to open and close naturally, with English mouth shapes. Lip sync.' T3: refs = a video with the right performance as video1 + keyframe image1; 'Reference [@video1]'s performance, lip movements, timing, camera movement and shot size exactly ... use [@image1] as the first frame'. Also: the 'First frame: use [@image1]' line locks a keyframe in reference mode. The English reading can come from any take with good speech (here the approved MiniMax H3 clip's sound). Source: Alibaba's official Wan 3.0 prompt guide (audio-driven, dialogue transfer). Rule: every Wan dialogue clip uses this audio-driven form; the user's own environment/look wording goes into the shot description.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: WAN DIALOGUE, BEST FORM SO FAR (user verdict 23 Nov 2026, test T6V on clip 3c: 'The shot 2 is good'): Wan 3.0 reference-to-video with FOUR references: the keyframe, the character sheet, the location picture, and a VIDEO of the same lines spoken with correct English lips (here the approved MiniMax H3 take). Prompt = the user's environment wording ('A medium close-up of the young woman from [@woman], as in [@keyframe]: standing before ... the feast hall from [@place] ... camera pushes in slowly ... eyes on the seated guests off screen right, never into the lens.') + 'Reference [@video1]'s performance, lip movements and timing exactly. She says, <manner>: "line" ... Lip sync. Her lips open and close exactly as in [@video1].' + the Audio/Only/Avoid lines ending 'NO native language, pure English dialogue, lip sync to the dialogue.' No first-frame lock. Result: lips right, her place in the room right, the Wan look the user likes. The same prompt with an AUDIO reference instead of the video (T6) also fixed the lips but put her on the wrong side of the guests: the video reference carries the staging as well as the lips. Cost note: this needs a reference take per dialogue clip (MiniMax H3 8 s = 40 credits) plus the Wan clip (48).

[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: WAN ENGLISH LIP SYNC, SIMPLEST WORKING FORM (user verdict 23 Nov 2026, test T11 on clip 4d-1, two speakers: 'the mouth works'; the user's own theory): Wan 3.0 reference-to-video with the KEYFRAME AS THE ONLY REFERENCE. Describe each person by their clothes and screen side ('The man on the LEFT wears a beige cotton jacket ...'), give every face an eye target and 'never into the lens', the camera move, then each line as 'The man in the beige jacket says, <manner>: "..." The man in the blue-grey robe replies, <manner>: "..." Lip sync. Whoever is not speaking keeps his mouth closed.', then the episode look line ('Look: photographic, 35mm film look, subtle grain, natural colour; warm lantern light from the left, cool blue moonlight outside ... No glamour, no heavy grade.'), ending 'No music. No subtitles or text. NO native language, pure English dialogue, lip sync to the dialogue.' Adding character sheets and a location picture is what broke the lips before. No audio, no reference video and no other model needed. Cost: Wan only (8 s = 48).
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: Wan dialogue standard: the keyframe is the ONLY reference (T11 form). People are described by their clothes and screen side, with eye targets, a camera move, the exact lines, 'Lip sync. Whoever is not speaking keeps his mouth closed.', the look line, and the ending 'No music. No subtitles or text. NO native language, pure English dialogue, lip sync to the dialogue.' Character sheets, location pictures, audio and reference videos broke the lips. User-confirmed 23 Nov 2026.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: G2b steward line (24 Nov 2026): mouth drifted. My prompt followed the T11 form but changed one thing that matters: I wrote 'head tipped down, eyes on the table below the frame', so his mouth was angled away and partly hidden. Rule: a speaking face is never tipped down; keep it level and near-frontal with the eyes on a target at eye height beside the lens (e.g. 'looks at the woman beside him, screen left'). Keep the full keyframe rather than a crop when possible.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-23: USER'S WORKING WAN PROMPT FORMAT (24 Nov 2026, 'Prompting in this way works'): direct Wan 3.0, keyframe only, no MiniMax reference. Structure: 'Single continuous cinematic shot, N seconds. The man in [@keyframe] ...'; a Shot paragraph (size, move, 'Preserve exact character appearance, positioning, clothing and environment.', lens, aperture); timecoded beats '00:00–00:01: ...' with the line as 'says in English: "..." Lip sync. <voice>, natural mouth articulation.' and a closing beat 'He closes his mouth'; Look line; 'Audio: English dialogue only, subtle ambience.'; 'No music, no narration, no voice-over, no additional dialogue, no other languages, no subtitles or on-screen text.' No clip over 7 seconds; fast pace.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-24: Episode 2 (24 Nov 2026): C15 and C18 mouths moved in another language, not English. Root cause: the Wan recipe template I wrote into the skill had dropped the user's language lock ('no other languages' and 'NO native language, pure English dialogue, lip sync to the dialogue'), which every approved Episode 1 dialogue prompt had; all Episode 2 prompts were built from that template. Fixed the template in both copies. Rule: every Wan dialogue prompt ends with the language lock; check for it before quoting.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-24: USER RULE 24 Nov 2026: every Wan dialogue prompt for The Man They Erased ends with the English speech lock, word for word: 'Speech language: English (en-US).' / 'The character speaks English naturally, with mouth movements precisely matching the audible English phonemes, syllables and speech rhythm.' / 'Negative prompt list: Korean-language articulation, dubbed speech, mismatched lip-sync, incorrect phonetic movements, unsynchronised audio.' Saved in the skill's Wan recipe.
[cards/clips.md] - 2026-11-24: Episode 3 C06 (24 Nov 2026): a three-word line with the voice note 'hard, low male voice' came out a near-whisper (a tenth of normal level) with the mouth barely moving. Regenerated asking for 'clearly and firmly, each word fully spoken', 'a strong, clear voice at full speaking volume, lips and jaw moving visibly on every word', and 'whispering, mumbling, closed-mouth speech' in the negative list: the user approved. Rule: never describe a Wan voice as low, quiet, under the breath or whispered; always ask for full speaking volume and visible articulation.
