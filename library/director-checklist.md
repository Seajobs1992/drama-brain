# LOCKED OUT: director's checklist

Written 16 November 2026 at the creator's request: a reminder I read before every episode, every scene setup and every batch of frames, and use to check every image before anything depends on it.

## Before an episode

- Re-read the episode's script, CONTINUITY-LOCKS.md and the previous episode's last frames.
- List every character, costume state, prop, wound, weather and time of day the scenes need. Note what changed since the last episode (bracelet on Wren's LEFT wrist from EP02, Deck's jacket lost EP11, Jonah's cut EP11, eye EP17, and so on).
- Build missing references first: face options (pick the best), full figure, three-panel sheet with the creator's template verbatim, prop references, empty location references. No frame before its references exist and pass.
- Write the scene map: compass directions, who stands where, the dialogue axis (Jonah screen-left facing right at the counter and the gate), where the light comes from.

## Choosing shots (directing)

- Every shot has a job: establish, reveal, react, insert a detail that matters. No decorative shots.
- Open each scene with a geography shot before close-ups.
- Speaker clips: one speaker per shot, face large enough for lip sync, eyeline toward the listener's side of the axis.
- Reactions show a change of mind, not just a face.
- Inserts carry story objects: keys, the red key, the bracelet, the ledger, the envelope, the file.
- Vary size and angle with purpose (wide, medium, close, insert, over-the-shoulder). Never cross the axis without a shot that re-establishes it.
- Over-the-shoulder shots: the foreground person is the listener, on their correct side of the counter, gate or table. Check this against the script every time (the 1-29 mistake).
- Physical action splits into setup, contact and result.

## Every prompt

- Image 1 is always the scene master for the place, layout and light. Then only the references the frame needs, each labelled ("Image 2 is X's exact face").
- Name hands anatomically, LEFT or RIGHT, and where they sit in the picture.
- Skin rules: "Photorealistic. A real photograph." Real skin texture, no glamorization. One named light direction. 35mm film look. Faces framed large.
- Red only for the red key's bow, the red kite and the Deep Room's lead seals.
- No text, numbers, logos or plates in images. Text is added in the edit.

## Checking every image (before it is used)

- Face matches the approved identity image: shape, eyes, hair, beard, age. No drift.
- Costume matches the sheet and the episode's state: colours, layers, damage, mud, wet.
- Props: same design as the reference, in the correct hand, on the correct side, in the correct state.
- Location matches the master: walls, doors, furniture, windows, light direction, time of day, weather.
- Positions and eyelines match the master and the neighbouring shots; the axis holds.
- Hands: five fingers, correct hand doing the action.
- No extra people, no missing people, no red where red is not allowed, no text.
- Never flip or mirror an image to fix anything. Regenerate or edit properly.
- Never assume: if something cannot be confirmed in the image, it fails until confirmed.
- Log every job: task ID only after it is returned, file, sha256, what passed, what failed, what was fixed.

## After an episode

- Contact board of every frame in shot order, with corrected frames marked.
- Update CONTINUITY-LOCKS.md with new cast, props, locations and state changes.
- Write the episode's Kling animation script (one speaker per clip, exact dialogue).
- Note credits spent and anything left for the creator to decide.
