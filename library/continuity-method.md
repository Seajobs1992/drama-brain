# Drama visual continuity

Use this skill before selecting references or generating the next drama image. Continue authorized work, but do not advance past a known continuity defect. The user should not have to identify basic staging mistakes.

For The Apothecary's Ledger, also read [the project reference map](references/apothecarys-ledger.md). Other projects use their own established references, not this cast or setting.

## Establish what controls each visible fact

Read the current script, shot plan, accepted changes, continuity notes and selected-image manifest. Distinguish user approval from an assistant's selection. A generated result is not approved merely because it exists.

Use this priority by responsibility:

1. Latest user correction controls the disputed fact and supersedes conflicting old plans.
2. The selected scene master controls positions, distances, architecture, background and light.
3. The relevant selected adjacent shot controls immediate pose, hand ownership and prop state.
4. Identity sheets control faces, age and proportions. Costume references control wardrobe and physical state.
5. Prop references control geometry, surfaces and construction.
6. Text descriptions fill gaps. They cannot replace an available visual reference.

Attach the actual files. For a close-up, make the scene master the primary spatial reference, then add character and prop detail references. Do not use an empty location sheet in place of the populated master. Do not use a costume portrait's incidental background as location authority. If tool slots are insufficient, use staged edits with inspection after each stage.

## Fix geography before making attractive compositions

Record a short scene map with physical anchors: entrances, seat, desk, stairs, mat, platform, evidence tray and each person. State who faces whom, distances, and camera side. Separate physical left/right from screen left/right.

Before each generation, write a compact shot check:

- Camera position and viewing direction relative to the master.
- Subject's unchanged position, knees/torso direction and gaze target.
- Background elements that should actually fall behind the subject from this camera.
- Visible and offscreen cast, with a reason for exclusions.
- Hands, carried objects and object state at this exact instant.
- Exact references and the limited responsibility of each.

A close-up magnifies a person in place. It must not move them into the yard or put distant architecture behind someone beside a platform. In reverse angles, a desk behind the camera cannot also appear behind the person facing it. Derive the new screen position of an object from the map. Never mirror an image to fix an eyeline or handedness.

Keep mat dimensions, orientation and distance to stairs unchanged across empty and populated views. When deriving an empty location from an accepted staging image, remove only the people requested; do not rebuild the set from prose. Inspect mat corners and surrounding distances after the edit.

Guards and crowds occupy physical positions. They do not move to fill background space. A narrow crop can exclude them if the geometry supports it. Do not add guards behind a person when the master places them nearer the camera. For important turns, preserve knees and seat position and distinguish a beginning turn from a completed turn. Avoid a dramatic side angle if it makes the person appear to have moved beside an entrance.

## Treat props as physical objects

Track anatomical hand ownership, orientation, scale, wear, binding, folds, knots and closures. Count visible construction details when they identify the prop. Do not claim a requested count was achieved without looking.

For an open book, prepare the inside spread AND the outside of the same open book. Closed front/back covers alone do not answer what the camera sees during reading. Pages face the reader; the opposite camera sees exterior covers. A page reveal requires a visible turn of the book before the reveal insert. Keep front/back wear and spine placement coherent; avoid a second binding at the outer page edge. Generated handwriting is not verified readable text.

Track state changes explicitly: closed to open, transfer, raised, rotated, withheld. Do not reveal later clues early or let objects teleport between hands or people. Preserve costume construction while changing only script-supported wetness, drying, mud or injury.

## Generate, inspect, then accept

Generate only within the user's current authorization. Use the project's chosen tool. Separate generation failures from successful images with visual defects; honor project retry rules. For an image with a defect, make a targeted correction grounded in the master, not a fresh broad prompt that can drift again.

Inspect the actual returned image against the master AND the relevant adjacent frame before recording it as selected:

- Same face and age, hair/tie/pin, clothing construction, mud and moisture state?
- Same position, scale, mat and architecture; plausible background depth and occlusion?
- Knees, torso, gaze and camera angle explain the intended target?
- Correct guards/crowd placement and no unplanned people?
- Correct hands, object faces, seams, stitch count, folds, knot, pouch side and reveal timing?
- One frozen starting instant rather than a mixture of action stages?

If any critical check fails, mark the candidate rejected and fix it before dependent shots. If a targeted correction repeatedly fails, change the constrained composition or method and explain the remaining issue. Do not keep calling it ready because generation succeeded. Tool previews may appear automatically; do not describe them as approved before inspection. Label scene context when a returning character could appear unexplained.

## Keep a clean, durable handoff

Save selected images immediately in the episode folder with stable story-order names. Save source paths, actual prompt/reference selections, checksums and the specific review outcome. Update the checklist only after files and behavior are verified. Do not depend on transient tool stores across interruptions.

Keep rejected candidates out of selected folders and galleries. Permanent deletion requires user authorization; when authorized, delete only confirmed rejected files and verify selected hashes before and after. Never delete every unlisted generation, because it may belong to another episode. Update superseded file references.

At handoff, distinguish completed images, remaining work and limitations. Do not claim perfect consistency or user approval. Preserve new user corrections in the project continuity notes so the next episode inherits them.
