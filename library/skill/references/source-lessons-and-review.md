# Film-derived lessons and review

## Evidence boundary

This edition builds on the earlier Untouchable Mechanic study and the thirteen-film Multi-Genre Study. The latter contains 583 source files including merged and backup variants, 1,424,365 indexed frames, 24,065 inspected candidate midpoint images and 207 film-specific mechanism entries. Those mechanism entries overlap across genres and are not 207 independently proven rules. The earlier study records 50 episodes, 1,991 candidate midpoint images and 111,011 indexed frames.

The studies used recognition text, subtitle evidence and sampled still images. Direct listening, complete caption adjudication, reliable attribution of every line, continuous motion and semantic inspection of every frame remain unfinished. Candidate intervals can include flashes and movement rather than editorial cuts. Do not claim to have verified voice performance, soundtrack timing, exact cutting rhythm or every spoken word from these materials.

The examples support narrative and sampled visual interpretations. They do not establish measured retention, creator intent or why a reported view count occurred. The skill works without the local library. Film titles and source-unit references are evidence pointers, not required dependencies. Merged sources mean a source unit is not always one editorial episode.

## Earlier Untouchable Mechanic examples

| Film example | Mechanism to reuse | What not to generalise |
|---|---|---|
| Episode 1 establishes Samuel's power before later disrespect | Audience knowledge can turn humiliation into anticipation of recognition | Every protagonist needs a secret elite identity |
| Episodes 7–9 connect the pendant gift, dismissal and public humiliation | A concrete object can carry relationship history and make harm legible | Every scene needs a symbolic prop |

Develop original plots, identities, dialogue and visual situations. Transfer the mechanism rather than reskinning the film scene for scene.

## Review by evidence, not decorative scores

For each significant issue, record:
- Location: episode, beat, shot or timestamp.
- Observation: the actual line, action, timing or state conflict.
- Consequence: why it weakens comprehension, anticipation, emotion or production reliability.
- Repair: a specific change within the requested scope.
- Verification: how to tell that the repair worked, and what remains unverified.

Useful failure tests:

**Weak hook:** Can the viewer identify the immediate question? Replace general exposition with a consequential situation, while retaining necessary context.

**Empty cliffhanger:** What committed action or specific discovery makes the cut matter? A startled face without a readable cause is insufficient.

**Repetitive escalation:** Do consecutive scenes change the available choices or merely repeat the same hostility? Change the constraint, cost or relationship.

**Unearned reversal:** What prior evidence supports the new power, skill, clue or ally? Seed it or revise the solution.

**Passive protagonist:** What does the lead choose, risk or refuse? Endurance needs a motive and a limit.

**Emotion without behaviour:** What can the actor visibly do? Specify an observable choice and let framing make it readable.

**Broken handoff:** Compare the last state of one shot or episode with the next opening. Resolve missing movements, moved objects, changed injuries or unexplained knowledge.

**Overloaded generation:** Can the primary action and spoken line fit with a readable reaction? Simplify coverage or split the action, preserving the approved dialogue and causal chain.

**Unsupported confidence:** Was a fact observed in the actual output, inferred from context or only requested in a prompt? Label it correctly.

When reviewing supplied material, do not invent evidence. When planning new material, describe audience response as an intention or hypothesis, not a promised performance result.
