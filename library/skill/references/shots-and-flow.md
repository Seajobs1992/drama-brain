# Shot direction and Flow handoff

## Stage relationships before selecting coverage

Identify the room layout, exits, main action axis, each person's position and the important object. Choose who faces whom and who can see or hear each revelation. Keep left/right screen positions and eyelines consistent unless the camera visibly re-establishes the geography.

Blocking can express status: who occupies the doorway, who must cross the room, who interrupts another person's reach, and who physically shields whom. Use these as character choices. Do not turn high and low angles into a mandatory moral code.

For a prop exchange, record its holder, hand, orientation, movement, contact and final location. For a wound, track side, costume damage, blood and physical limitation. A new close-up must inherit the previous state.

## Coverage with a purpose

An establishing view can make a later close-up intelligible. An insert should expose a relevant detail. A reaction should show a changed interpretation. A two-shot can preserve a relationship or power imbalance better than alternating isolated faces.

Choose camera movement only when it changes access to information, follows purposeful action, reveals geography or alters emotional proximity. State one dominant camera instruction. Avoid combining a static camera, orbit, dolly and zoom in one short clip.

For physical action, show enough preparation, direction, contact and consequence to make the cause readable. Split a complex exchange into manageable shots rather than asking for multiple attacks, disarms and falls in one generation. Do not conceal incoherent action with arbitrary rapid cuts.

## Timing and sound

Keep each spoken line assigned to its actual speaker, with exact wording and spoken language. Distinguish audible dialogue, voiceover, inner thought and nonverbal sound. Describe intention through playable delivery: “tries to sound casual; hesitates before the name”, not “very dramatic”.

Estimate speaking time, then add pauses, action and reaction. A rough planning range of 120–170 words per minute can expose an overloaded English line, but it is not a model limit or a substitute for a read-through. Language, emotion and interruptions change duration. If it does not fit, flag the conflict and propose splitting the shot or an authorised script edit. Never silently paraphrase approved dialogue.

Specify motivated ambience, effects and score intention separately. Silence or a held reaction can carry a reveal. Avoid constant impact sounds under every sentence. Do not promise that a generator will produce exact dialogue, lip sync or sound mixing. Verify the output afterward when audio access is available.

## Copyable shot packet

Use existing shot IDs and project records. For each requested shot provide:

**Shot:** ID, episode and dramatic purpose.
**Timing:** Planned edit duration and spoken/action windows. Distinguish this from supported generation length.
**References:** Actual inspected files, their roles and order. Mark unavailable assets as missing; never invent a path or imply a reference was attached.
**Start state:** Character appearance, costume, positions, eyelines, visible props and location.
**Performance and action:** One primary action with clear cause and destination; necessary secondary reaction.
**Camera:** Framing, angle, lens feel if useful, focus target and one motivated movement or locked camera.
**Dialogue:** Speaker, exact line, language, delivery and whether on-camera, offscreen or voiceover.
**Sound:** Ambience, specific effects and score intention, where relevant.
**End state:** Final positions, expression, object location and intended editorial cut.
**Continuity locks:** The facts most likely to drift in this shot.

Then supply one clean prompt block containing the executable direction. Keep critique, rationale and missing-asset notes outside it. Do not bury alternatives inside the prompt.

## Flow-specific preparation

Before actual submission, discover the available Flow tools and inspect the selected model/mode. Verify supported duration, image/reference inputs, continuation, audio support and costs from the actual tool or current authoritative documentation. Do not hard-code an assumed eight-second limit or guaranteed feature.

If capabilities are unknown, still prepare the directing packet and label the prompt as a draft awaiting capability checks. Use verified character and location references where the chosen workflow supports them. A planned reference is not a generated asset. Text-to-video and image-to-video are different workflows; preserve the user's chosen one.

For continuation, distinguish a true continuous camera view from a new editorial angle. Use the actual prior output and end frame when supported and available. Do not claim a seamless continuation using a hypothetical final frame. For a new angle, preserve the physical state while deliberately resetting the camera.

After authorised generation, compare the actual clip against the approved start/end states, action, exact dialogue and continuity. Mark checks as passed, failed or unverified. A prompt that contains the correct line is not evidence that the output said it. Repair the smallest failing unit within the authorised budget and retry scope.
