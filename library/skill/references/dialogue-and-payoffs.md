# Dialogue, emotional change and payoffs

## Write tactics rather than explanations

For each important exchange, identify what each speaker wants the other person to do now. Useful tactics include bargaining, concealing, testing, reassuring, deflecting, exposing and refusing. Change the tactic when the response defeats it. Do not label every delivery angry or emotional.

Give a recurring character a compact voice pattern: direct or indirect, formal or casual, preferred tactic, vulnerable subject, and what changes under pressure. Do not reduce identity to a catchphrase. Keep children distinct without making every child a miniature adult.

A line should change a belief, force a choice, reveal a contradiction, protect a secret or deepen attachment. Remove explanation already legible in the image unless repetition itself creates a new meaning. Let the answer cause the next line rather than writing alternating speeches.

## Useful forms

- **Concrete test:** Replace “Will you always support me?” with a situation in which support has a cost. The answer can be an action or revealing hesitation.
- **Subtext through a task:** A character meticulously returns a key while claiming the separation is temporary. The object and behavior may contradict the words.
- **Changed repetition:** Repeat a promise only when the speaker, cost, listener or consequence changes.
- **Evidence before verdict:** Let a character point to a date, missing signature or object state before delivering the accusation.
- **Silence with a decision:** State what the silence permits or refuses. Do not use a pause as empty padding.

These are options, not mandatory line templates. Prefer character-specific speech over polished slogans everyone could say.

## Keep dialogue and sound channels distinct

For a script, assign every line to a character and distinguish on-camera speech, offscreen speech, narration and inner thought. An animal’s private voice must not accidentally become audible to everyone. Identify who can hear each revelation.

For source reconstruction, use an unassigned speaker when evidence is insufficient. Keep OCR and recognition alternatives separate until adjudicated. Do not turn uncertain transcription into confident canon.

For production, preserve approved wording and language. Budget speech plus breaths, interruptions, action and reaction. Do not solve an overloaded clip by quietly removing a line. Split the shot or propose a revised line when script revision is authorized.

## Payoff ledger

Use this only to the level the project needs:

| Setup | Promise to viewer | Preparation | Delivery | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A worker keeps every delivery receipt | Her diligence may become useful | The records settle a small dispute early | A later receipt contradicts a false accusation | Planned |
| A friend promises to respect a refusal | Trust depends on conduct | A boundary is stated clearly | A repeated request is declined and the friend accepts it | Planned |

These are original illustrative setups, not claims about a studied film. Mark delivery only after it appears in the actual script or finished output being assessed.

Distinguish four stages: requested, promised, attempted and completed. A refund email that promises payment is not a completed refund. A rescue plan is not a rescue. A proposal is not a wedding. This distinction makes both suspense and closure more precise.

## Emotional arcs without compulsory forgiveness

Track the starting belief, experience that challenges it, decision under pressure and changed behavior. Do not infer completed reform from a speech, a gift or a single rescue. Conversely, do not erase an actual positive action merely because it falls short of restoring trust.

The harmed person can accept help, acknowledge effort, forgive, reconcile or choose none of these. State which occurs. In an emancipation story, ending alone can fulfill the promise. In a romance, mutual choice should be legible even when the characters remain flawed.

## Revision pass

Check whether adjacent lines repeat the same accusation or answer. Preserve repetition that changes meaning; replace filler with a new fact, decision, cost or physical action. Read the exchange for speaking time and distinguish estimated timing from an actual recorded performance.

At the ending, revisit promises outside the central relationship: a death investigation, public accusation, estate, competition, creature bond or professional restriction. Resolve them, deliberately leave them open, or revise the setup within authorized scope. Do not label an abandoned thread a payoff.
