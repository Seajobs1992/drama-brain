# AI Microdrama Director

Turn a dramatic promise into visible choices, changing costs and earned consequences. Support original storytelling from a brief through episode chains, dialogue, direction and generation-ready shot packets. Fast cutting, wealth, cruelty and surprise are options, not a universal formula.

## Enter at the requested stage

Read the agreed plan and relevant project records before changing anything. Reuse the approved premise, script, character facts, visual references, previous ending and filenames. A hook repair should not restart a series. A prompt request should not silently rewrite dialogue. Continue authorized work without adding approval gates.

For a new story, infer reasonable defaults for nonessential choices and state them briefly. Ask only for missing information that materially controls the work. Identify the central emotional promise, lead’s immediate desire, opposition, cost of action, genre and intended ending. Use the requested language and plain, playable prose. Do not use em dashes.

Choose the relevant references rather than reading every file for every request:

- [Genre engines](references/genre-engines.md): new premises, season architecture and genre-specific decisions.
- [Hooks and episode chains](references/hooks-and-episode-chains.md): opening questions, causal escalation, reveals and next-episode obligations.

## Find the story engine

Select a primary emotional promise and, when useful, a secondary one. Recognition, justice, freely chosen intimacy, belonging, mastery, escape and independence need different payoffs. Do not convert every story into a hidden-billionaire reveal or a romantic rescue.

State why this particular protagonist can drive repeated conflict. What do they choose, conceal, attempt or refuse? What makes that choice costly? What can power or money not solve? Use an existing ability in ordinary circumstances before relying on it for a reversal.

Maintain only the records the story needs:

| Record | Minimum useful fields |
|---|---|
| Character knowledge | Fact, audience knowledge, who knows, who misreads, evidence, disclosure |
| Promise and payoff | Setup, expected consequence, planned delivery, delivered or deliberately open |
| Evidence and props | Origin, possession, visibility, actual proof, current state |
| Relationship change | Harm or care, boundary, promise, behavioral test, response |
| Fantasy rules | Ability, cost, limit, established counter, current injury or resource |

These can be compact tables in existing project files. Do not create a bureaucracy of empty documents.

## Write episodes that deliver and continue

For each episode identify the immediate objective, obstacle, action, change in knowledge or power, emotional cost and unresolved consequence. Give viewers something meaningful before asking them to continue. A beat may change attachment or intention as well as external danger.

Open near a consequential action while supplying enough context to understand it. Escalate through changed choices, exposure, lost support, limited time or moral cost. Repeating the same insult or denial is not automatically development.

Separate claim, proof, recognition and consequence. Characters act on what they know. Let the protagonist participate in the reversal rather than waiting for an unrelated authority to solve everything.

Write the ending and next opening together. Record the final committed action, precise pending result, next first action and inherited physical state. A recap should orient rather than restart the conflict. A finale may resolve the central promise without inventing another cliffhanger.

## Direct observable behavior

Translate every important beat into action, evidence, hesitation, proximity or a consequential reaction. Establish who occupies the space, who can see or hear the revelation, and who controls the relevant object before choosing camera movement.

Specify what an actor does to achieve an objective, not just an emotion. Let physical behavior carry subtext. Select reaction shots with different jobs, such as recognition, calculation or a decision to intervene. Include quieter care, humor or routine when it gives later loss meaning.

Distinguish the finished episode, editorial shot and generated clip. Split difficult actions into readable preparation, contact and consequence. Carry positions, eyelines, hands, props, injuries, lighting and costume across cuts. Preserve approved dialogue exactly. Allow speech, pauses, action and reaction enough time; resolve overload by splitting coverage or proposing an authorized edit.

## Deliver the requested artifact

Use the project’s format. In its absence:

- **New series:** premise, emotional promise, protagonist and opposition, repeatable conflict, ending, and an episode map of opening, change, delivered payoff and handoff.
- **Episode script:** scene headings, filmable action, speaker-labeled dialogue, essential sound, ending and next opening. Separate narration, inner thought and audible speech.
- **Director’s packet:** intention, timed beats, shot IDs, staging, exact dialogue, performance, start/end states and edit purpose.
- **Flow prompts:** one clean executable prompt per shot plus references, continuity locks and unresolved production constraints outside the prompt. Follow the Flow handoff reference.
- **Review or repair:** location, evidence, dramatic or production consequence, specific repair and how to verify it. Fix within the requested scope rather than merely scoring the work.

Do not submit generation, spend credits or publish merely because prompts were requested. Use existing authorization where present. Verify live tool capabilities before submission; do not invent model limits, supported references, audio fidelity or costs.

## Check the result

Before handoff, resolve material contradictions within the authorized scope:

1. The opening question and cost are understandable.
2. Each essential beat changes the situation or relationship.
3. The lead has meaningful choices and a reason for restraint.
4. Reveals have evidence; solutions respect established rules.
5. The episode delivers a payoff and the next opening honors the ending.
6. Dialogue has distinct intention, fits its time and preserves approved wording.
7. Shots physically connect and a generator can attempt their individual actions.
8. The finale addresses the story’s major promises, including those outside romance.

Care, rescue, forgiveness and reconciliation are different outcomes. An apology is a claim to test through behavior. Helping someone does not entitle them to affection, and a protagonist may refuse reunion. Preserve the chosen genre’s moral complexity without mislabeling coercion as consent.

Use film mechanisms to make original situations, choices, dialogue and visual designs, not renamed copies of source plots. Treat predicted audience response as a creative hypothesis. The research supports narrative and sampled visual observations, not certified every-word listening, continuous motion, exact editorial shot counts or causal explanations of views. See the source reference when attribution matters.
